## What I did

Correlation - when two variables are related to each other, meaning when one variable changes, the other variabletends to change.
HOwever, that doesnt mean that one variable is responsible for the  change in other.
EX- Icecream sales and temperature
1. When temperature increases, icecream sales also increase.
2. When temperature decreases, icecream sales also decrease.
Hence, icecream sales and temperature increase has +ve correlation.
ALso, icecream sales doesnot  cause the temperature to increase.

There are 3 types of  correlation:-
1. Positive correlation-   When both variables are moving in the same direction. THat means both variable are completely correlated to each other.
EX- Studying for more hrs eventually can give higher marks.
2. Negative correlation-  Variables move in opposite direction.
EX- Higher product prices lowers the customer demand.
3. Zero correlation- No clear linear relationship in between the variables.
EX- Shoe size and exam marks.

Caussation- Means when one variable directly influences or produces a change in another variable.
Simple meaning- one thing happens bcz of another thing.
EX- Studying hrs and exam performance
A student studies more, understands the concepts, and their exam performance improves.
Here, studying can contribute to better exam performance.

## What I learned

Insight- COrrelation defines a relationship in between variables but doesnt prove  any cause and effect thing and can happen due to coincidence or other factor.
Caussation on the other hand, it is influential, and shows cause and effect relationship.

## Next

INterview QUEs asked-  
Suppose you observe that people who drink more coffee tend to work for longer hours.
SO, can you conclude that drinking coffee can cause people to work longer?

Coorelation:-  Workload and coffee consumption increase together
Causation:-   If increased workload actually leads employees to consume more coffee.

Imagine two employees:

- Employee A has a heavy workload and drinks 4 cups of coffee daily.
- Employee B has a light workload and drinks 1 cup of coffee daily.

You observe that as workload increases, coffee consumption also increases.

This indicates a positive correlation.

But does workload cause people to drink more coffee? Not necessarily. It might be because:

- Heavy workload makes employees feel tired.
- They drink more coffee to stay awake.
- Other factors, such as sleep patterns, may also influence coffee consumption.
